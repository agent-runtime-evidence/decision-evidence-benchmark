# Responsible AI — DEMM-Bench

This document records the intended use, out-of-scope uses, limitations, and misuse risks of the DEMM-Bench benchmark and dataset. It complements the dataset card (`README.md`) and the datasheet (`DATASHEET.md`).

## Intended use

DEMM-Bench is a measurement instrument for **governance-evidence sufficiency**: it scores whether the records an agent-runtime system persists are sufficient to reconstruct decision-level properties for a specific decision, under controlled evidence degradation. It is intended for:

- benchmarking property-level sufficiency scorers against container-presence baselines;
- comparative measurement of **Overclaim Rate** across evidence regimes and degradation conditions;
- research on post-incident decision reconstruction and audit-evidence design.

## Out of scope (non-goals)

DEMM-Bench does **not** measure, and must not be presented as measuring:

- **legal adequacy or regulatory conformance** — these depend on jurisdiction, facts, and qualified assessment;
- **production prevalence** — the corpus is a controlled construction-oracle dataset, not a naturalistic incident sample, so results do not establish how often these failures occur in deployed systems;
- **task success, capability, or safety** — those are left to adjacent benchmark families;
- **certification of any scorer** (including the reference Decision Trace Reconstructor) as a production audit tool;
- **replay of an LLM's hidden reasoning** under altered weights or prompts.

Passing the benchmark shows a scorer detects property-level evidence gaps under controlled conditions; it does not establish legal adequacy, operational safety, or regulatory compliance.

## Data provenance and ethics

- **Synthetic, incident-framed.** Scenarios are synthetic, derived from published incident framings (e.g., AI Incident Database entries). They are framed by real incident patterns but are **not field-captured production truth**.
- **No human/animal subjects; no personal data.** No human or animal subjects research was conducted, no personal data is processed, and IRB review was not required.
- **Deterministic labels.** Ground-truth property labels are generated by a versioned construction-oracle rule file with paired-oracle self-consistency (deterministic rule reproducibility), not by human annotation.

## Limitations

- **Construction-oracle labels are not field truth.** They support controlled measurement of overclaim under known missingness, conflict, and structural-unfillability transformations, but do not establish empirical failure frequency in deployed systems.
- **Baselines are intentionally weak lower-bound controls.** The five container-presence baselines are documented lower bounds; a stronger property-level scorer could plausibly reduce overclaim below the reported values. The benchmark claim is comparative against a documented lower bound, not absolute against the best possible alternative.
- **Agreement is internal reproducibility, not human agreement.** The paired-oracle agreement diagnostic shows the construction-oracle rules execute reproducibly; it does not prove the property taxonomy is semantically unambiguous or that human inter-rater agreement would be high.
- **Bounded scorer and regime scope.** The eight evidence regimes do not exhaust future substrates, and the evaluated scorer set tests the container-presence class, not every governance evaluator.

## Misuse risks and mitigations

Two misuse risks are anticipated:

- **Benchmark gaming** — maximizing Property Sufficiency Accuracy on the deposited cases without genuine reconstruction capacity, then advertising that score as production-audit certification.
- **Weaponized framing** — quoting headline overclaim figures to push proprietary scorers that were not themselves evaluated against the same property-level oracle.

**Mitigations:**

1. Publish all construction-oracle rules and degradation transformations for independent replay.
2. Scope every quantitative claim to the specific deterministic package it was measured on (the 64-case package).
3. Attach the limitations above and the explicit non-goals to every external communication of a result.
